fix(app): say what actually happens to a request

Every form told the truth and every confirmation did not. Beside each submit
control the forms read `Fixture only — nothing is sent off this device`. After
submitting, a person read that the Indianapolis office answers their inquiry,
that print and digital delivery is recorded for them, or that an invoice was
recorded — with the correction placed in a continuation block further down the
page, after the sentence that already told them it worked.

A person reads the confirmation. The confirmation now carries the truth.

    No inquiry was sent
    This site is a demonstration. Nothing reached the Indianapolis office, and
    nobody will answer this inquiry. What you typed is below, and it stays in
    this browser.

The subscribe and payment desks say the same for what they do. Each keeps the
entered fields, which are real, and stops promising the action nobody takes.
The heading constants carried the promise — `You are subscribed` — so the
values changed rather than gaining a sentence underneath.

The refusals carried the same promise in the refusal's clothes. `The
subscription could not be sent` implies a valid entry would be sent, and no
entry ever is: a probe over the real controller found no storage key written
after three submits, and no `fetch`, `sendBeacon`, `WebSocket`, or
`EventSource` call exists anywhere under `app/`. Each refusal now names what
refused and what it refused — the form, and the entries — using the word this
repository already uses for the state.

Forms that collect a name, a company, an email address, and a phone number now
say what happens to it, above the fields rather than under the submit control,
so a person reads it before typing rather than after. The notice states only
what the probe established: it stays in this browser, it is not sent anywhere,
and the page discards it on close or reload. It claims no retention schedule,
no third parties, and no rights-request address, because this application
performs none of that and a notice describing handling that does not happen is
the same untruth at greater length.

The notice lives in the form components, so the newsletter desk inherits it
without its own edit.

A subscribe heading swung between two values by tense — `What the subscription
carries` before acceptance, `Recorded with this subscription` after. The tense
was the promise, so the derived label is gone and the heading describes the
real subscription in either state.

`guides/README.md` stated the retired confirmations as product truth in four
passages, the retired refusal in a fifth, and carried `Recorded` as a second
vocabulary for the entered fields in its data-states table. All are corrected.

Every changed sentence a test asserted was updated in the same change, and each
confirmation gained a negative binding on the promise it retired. A tree-wide
sweep finds every retired sentence only inside those negative bindings.
