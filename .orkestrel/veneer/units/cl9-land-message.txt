Ship the table key (CL9)

The published cascade carries the table classes: the base table with its
cells and sections, the compact and bordered and borderless variants, the
striped rows and striped columns, the active and hover states, the eight
contextual roles, the group divider, the caption class, and the responsive
wrappers unconditioned and at every breakpoint. Every selector the pinned
record carries under the key ships at its condition, nothing extra, nothing
deferred, with a selector row per family and a variable row for each of the
fourteen custom properties.

This is the first key recorded under maximum widths, so it is the downward
breakpoint mixin's first production consumer. The mixin stays as it is: it
emits the modern range form where the record carries the older spelling with
a fractional boundary, the two select the same viewports, and the equivalence
belongs in the comparison. That equivalence is arithmetic rather than a
spelling rewrite — the record's boundary is the named one less two
hundredths — and an audit lane verified the rounding in binary64 at every
recorded boundary.

Teaching the comparison that direction exposed a defect in a function every
key's presence scan uses. Its guard against rewriting inside a legacy
pseudo-element tested whether the preceding character was a colon but not
whether that colon was escaped, while the guard immediately after it tested
both. An escaped colon in a class name therefore blocked the rewrite and left
two spellings of one selector distinct. The guard now matches its neighbour,
and a case drives the real presence scanner to pin it.

The contextual colours derive from Veneer's role tokens rather than the
record's fixed values, which is recorded as a departure with browser controls
that restore the record's own inputs — so a changed record surfaces rather
than being absorbed. The role loop reads the token module's Bootstrap-aligned
list, which is the only one the vocabulary comparison permits: the broader
list carries a role the record does not.

Every gate exits 0 on managed Chromium and Edge. The audit is recorded in
scaffold's .orkestrel/veneer/cl9-audit-verdict.md file.
