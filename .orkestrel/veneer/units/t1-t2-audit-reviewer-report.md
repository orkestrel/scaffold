## Findings outside the claims

The sentence reads "`isReachable` is the one reachability filter the layer applies — `resolveRendered`, `clickAccessibleWithin`, and `clickDisclosure` each narrow their own candidates and then keep the ones it accepts". **F3 — one media axis carries a different word in the key, the local binding, and the member doc, and `forced` is bound to two referents inside `stageMedia`.**
`/home/user/test/src/browser/types.ts` declares the key `forced` and documents it as "Omit it to leave the mode alone", while its own summary and the guide row at `guides/test.md:264` call the axis "forced colours". Inside the function that implements it, `helpers.ts:3126` binds `const colors = options.forced` and `helpers.ts:3133` binds `const forced = matchMedia('(forced-colors: active)').matches`. Why it matters: `AGENTS.md` § Design laws fixes one term per concept, and here the public key's own word names the pre-call reading in the function implementing that key, so a reader of `helpers.ts:3154-3169` must hold two bindings for `forced` and remember that `colors` is the option. The sibling axes do not do this: the option keeps the option's word (`print`, `motion`) and the reading takes its own (`media`, `reduced`). This axis inverts that, and the inversion is what forced the invented `colors`. What right looks like: bind `const forced = options.forced`, rename the pre-call reading for the state it names — `const active = matchMedia('(forced-colors: active)').matches`, carried into the `MEDIA_STAGE` array at `:3137` and the `forced-colors` feature entry at `:3146-3148` — and change the member doc in `types.ts` to "Omit it to leave the colours alone", matching the noun its own summary uses.

**F4 — `holdAccessibleWithin` does not scroll an off-viewport target, and the guide asserts the symmetric reading.**
`resolveAccessible` scrolls a wholly off-viewport target into view and refuses what stays outside (`helpers.ts:361-372`); `resolveAccessibleWithin` does neither (`helpers.ts:451-472`), and `driveHold` maps the target's `getBoundingClientRect()` centre straight into page coordinates (`helpers.ts:671-675`). So the scoped hold and the document-wide hold diverge on an off-viewport control. The guide states the opposite at `guides/test.md:439-442`: the scoped verbs "drive the control it resolves the way `holdAccessible` and `traverseAccessible` drive a document-wide one", with the only stated difference being the twin. The bounds row for the hold (`guides/test.md:1645-1651`) says "The verb inherits the resolver's focus reachability conditions" and names only `holdAccessible`. Why it matters: the guide sells the scoped verb as the same act at a narrower scope, and a consumer holding a control below the fold gets a different outcome depending on which verb it chose, with no sentence telling it so. The traversal pair does not have this asymmetry — `traverseAccessible` resolves through `resolveRendered`, which also does not scroll. What right looks like: name the difference in the bounds row, and drop or qualify the equivalence clause at `guides/test.md:441-442` so it claims scoping alone. The runtime half is referred to the objective lane (R2).

**F5 — the composition is documented in one direction only.**
`holdAccessibleWithin` and `traverseAccessibleWithin` each carry an `@remarks` naming `{@link driveHold}` or `{@link driveTraversal}` (`helpers.ts:627-630`, `:851-853`), while `holdAccessible` (`helpers.ts:571-591`) and `traverseAccessible` (`helpers.ts:825-836`) name neither. A consumer who called `holdAccessible`, read its refusal, and looked it up lands on a function its own doc block never mentions. The attribution change is right; the return path is missing. What right looks like: one clause in each of those two doc blocks naming the drive it composes, the way the scoped siblings do. While there, note that `holdAccessible`'s `@throws` still lists the resolver refusal before the held-pointer refusal (`helpers.ts:576-577`) while `holdAccessibleWithin`'s lists them in the order the code refuses (`helpers.ts:624-625`); the older text was already loose and the change made the family inconsistent about it.
