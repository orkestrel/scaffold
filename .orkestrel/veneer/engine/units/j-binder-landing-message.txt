Land J-BINDER: the shared mechanisms every engine builds on, with ownership and restoration under reactions

J-BINDER adapts the engine's shared mechanisms to the landed contracts: `HostSnapshot` (renamed
from `Snapshot`) records host state per target and writes it back in a fixed order (class tokens,
then each `class` attribute it recorded as absent and the tokens left empty, then inline
properties, then attributes), and hands a pending original to any snapshot saved during its
restoration through a class-static pending registry that the restoring snapshot owns, withdrawing
each target as soon as it writes it back, so a replacement engine constructed by a reaction
records none of the destroyed engine's writes; `Button` releases its claim before restoring and
re-reads its lifetime and the host after each reaction-capable write and dispatch, so a reaction
that destroys or re-enters it leaves the host and its events consistent; `ColorMode` does the
same for its attribute and storage writes; `Delegate` drops its hold, drives at once, marks each
click per engine class, and stops observing when no owned engine remains; `resolveOptions` reads
each entity's attribute table (`AttributeMap`) with a parser map (`ParserMap`) and no prefix,
`resolveVocabulary` validates the class, attribute, and selector groups with `isClassToken`,
`isAttributeName`, and `isSelector`, and `recordCalls` records prototype calls for the browser
tests. The guide's § Ownership and restoration states the handoff, the precedence (the
restoration that started first writes a shared target), and the write-sequence rule with its two
doors (a custom element's attribute reaction inside a write; a synchronous listener inside a
dispatch). The app's button section and its tests follow the renamed selectors. Every proof read
red first; the instrument's mutation rows redden each. Six rounds, each audited by Astra on the
objective lane, Opus 5.5 on the subjective lane, and the checker, with the Orchestrator's gates and
mutation replays. J-ENGINE campaign (D43).
