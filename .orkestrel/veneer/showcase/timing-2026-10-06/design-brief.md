# Design brief — task 75: the timing-sensitive journey readings

## Objective

Decide, for each of eight timing-sensitive readings in veneer's browser suites, what the wait or window is for (its property), what signal ends it, and how its bound is sized, so that a run on this host (Linux, Chromium 141.0.7390.37, four journey projects serialized behind one host lock) passes without a budget chosen by eye and without hiding a real defect. The result is one verdict with bounded implementation units for an `astra` lane. Rows and Journal entries must stay deterministic across runs, because the journey compare (`tmp/units/journey-cost/compare.ts`) gates every landing on row equality against the J-B2 pair (`runs/jb2b-1`, `jb2b-2`; 94 registered, 0 skipped, on veneer `9bc0003`).

## Rules the design must keep

- A budget is a property with a measurement behind it, never a fixed figure chosen for comfort (`/home/user/scaffold/.claude/rules/writing.md` § Instruction files; `AGENTS.md` § Writing). Where a figure stays, it is a contract (a protocol limit, an engine constant) or a measurement with its run named.
- A wait ends on a signal the engine or the browser emits, or on a settle predicate, not on a sleep.
- A refusal check proves nothing changed; its window must be at least as long as the longest transition the refused action could have started, and no longer than a measured settle plus its margin.
- A reading row (the `rows` array and `console.info` Journal entries) carries counts and values only; elapsed time lives in its own entry carrying `seconds` or `milliseconds`, which the compare excludes (`compare.ts:50-61`, `:101-107`).
- One writer per checkout; every Chromium or CPU-loading command through the host queue (`flock /home/user/.wave/journey.lock node tmp/units/journey-cost/run.ts`), fresh folder per run; a filtered or non-comparing journey run takes `--kind command`.

## Questions the design answers

1. Which of readings 1 to 6 are budgets (a bound on a wait), which are windows (a bound on an observer), and which are read costs (no wait at all)? For each: the property, the ending signal or settle predicate, and the sizing rule (a measured settle on this host at the slow end of the band, plus a margin stated as a property).
2. For reading 6: is a 5 ms window with one frame the right refusal observation when motion is off? What late mutation can a refused escape on a static-backdrop modal produce (the engine's `static` shake class, an `aria-*` write, a focus trap write), and must the observer wait for the engine's own completion signal instead of a clock?
3. For reading 7: what signal says the previous state's panel has left the document, and where does the reader wait for it (before `buildJourney`, before the count, or by counting only connected elements the current state owns)? Must `excluded` stay in the row at all, or is it a Journal count?
4. For reading 3 and the shared table timeout: must each table carry its own bound sized from its own scenario count and measured settles, and must the timeout be a property of `COMPONENT_TABLES` rows?
5. For reading 4: must `waitForAnimations` take a budget sized from the measured settle (774.5 ms of 1,000 ms at `:3199`), and what is the right margin property under the band of reading 8?
6. For reading 5: is the 8-poll settle and the 100-press bound the right predicate, or must the reader wait for the engine's scrollspy activation signal?
7. Which of these are veneer test-layer changes, which touch `@orkestrel/test/browser` (the `waitForAnimations` default; owned by the desktop session's packages), and which need an engine signal (a `hidden` lifecycle for tips) that stage B owns?

## Output

A verdict file with: one ruling per question; per reading, the property, signal, and sizing rule; the bounded units in order with owned files, acceptance criteria (each a gate or a measurement with its run), and what each unit must not change; the records to amend. Then the Orchestrator writes the Astra briefs.
